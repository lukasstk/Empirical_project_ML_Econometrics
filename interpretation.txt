================================================================================
INTERPRETATION OF RESULTS
DML analysis of urban emission policies (congestion pricing CP, low-emission
zone LEZ) on log transport CO2. Baseline: DoubleML PLR, plugin lasso
(hdm::rlasso) nuisances, city-level folds, cluster-robust SEs, n_rep = 5.
================================================================================

1. MAIN EFFECTS (tab_main_effects_dml.csv, tab_policy_regimes.csv)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                    log points     percent           p-value
  CP alone            -0.180       ~ -16%  [-31%, +1%]   0.057
  LEZ alone           +0.289       ~ +34%  [+16%, +54%]  <0.001
  CP x LEZ (synergy)  -0.944                             <1e-6
  Both vs. none       -0.776       ~ -54%  [-66%, -38%]  <1e-6

- CP alone: a reduction of roughly 16% in transport CO2, borderline
  significant at the 5% level. The magnitude is in the range documented for
  real-world congestion charges (roughly -10% to -20% traffic in the zone),
  so it is plausible on its face.
- LEZ alone: a 34% INCREASE. Substantively, a small positive effect would be
  defensible (LEZs target PM/NOx, not CO2; drivers may detour around the
  zone), but +34% is far too large to be a causal effect of a low-emission
  zone. See the placebo test below: this coefficient is most plausibly
  selection bias, not a policy effect.
- Interaction: strongly negative and highly significant. The joint effect is
  much larger than the sum of the individual effects (super-additive
  synergy). Answer to question 3: joint implementation is MORE effective
  than the sum of its parts.
- Both vs. none (regime parametrization): -54%, estimated directly as a
  single coefficient with its own CI.

Consistency check across the two parametrizations:
  theta_cp + theta_lez + theta_int = -0.18 + 0.29 - 0.94 = -0.83
  vs. directly estimated "both" coefficient          = -0.78
  These agree within sampling noise; the two runs are mutually consistent.

2. PLACEBO TEST (tab_placebo_announcement.csv) -- THE KEY RESULT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  cp_pre  = +0.33  (p = 0.001)
  lez_pre = +0.39  (p < 0.0001)

In city-years where a policy was ANNOUNCED (major topic of public
discussion) but NOT YET ACTIVE, emissions are 33-39 log points HIGHER than
the controls predict. Since no charge is collected and no vehicle is banned
in these years, the policy cannot mechanically cause this. The reading is:
cities adopt these policies because their emissions are high or rising --
selection on the outcome that the control set W does not absorb.
Conditional ignorability (the identifying assumption) therefore fails.

Direction of the bias: UPWARD for both policies.
- The LEZ pre-gap (+0.39) is about the same size as the estimated LEZ
  "effect" (+0.29). The positive LEZ coefficient is therefore plausibly
  entirely selection; the true LEZ effect is around zero or mildly negative.
- The CP estimate (-0.18) is likewise biased upward; the true CP effect is
  likely MORE negative (back-of-envelope: -0.18 minus a pre-gap of ~0.33
  puts it near -0.5 log points).

This is the headline finding for the report: the estimator checks all pass
(section 3), but the identification check does not, and it signs the bias.

3. SENSITIVITY (tab_sensitivity_specs.csv)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Learner choice (plugin lasso, CV lasso 1se/min, ridge, random forest):
  signs never flip, magnitudes stay in a band (CP: -0.15 to -0.35, LEZ:
  +0.17 to +0.46, interaction: -0.80 to -1.16). The random forest pulls CP
  down and LEZ up moderately -- worth a sentence, not a concern.
- Control sets: adding the mediators (pm25, fleet shares) cuts the LEZ
  coefficient roughly in half (+0.29 -> +0.14). This is exactly the
  bad-control / mediator-absorption logic that motivated excluding them
  from the baseline: part of the effect operates through the fleet, and
  conditioning on the channel removes it. It confirms the baseline choice.
- Folds (2 vs. 5 vs. 10): estimates stable, as DML theory predicts.

4. HETEROGENEITY (tab_heterogeneity_dml.csv)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CP: significant negative interactions with GDP per capita (-0.62, p=0.03),
population density (-0.21, p=0.04), public transit quality (-0.028 per
index point, p=0.01) and fuel price (-3.12 per EUR/l, p=0.001; the fuel
price range is only 1.34-1.74 EUR, so this is ~-0.23 per SD).
Economically sensible: pricing works better where alternatives exist
(transit, density) and where it stacks on an existing price signal
(fuel price). Answer to question 2: dense, richer cities with good public
transport benefit the most from CP.

LEZ: no significant heterogeneity in any characteristic.

Consistency with the main run: in the heterogeneity run the CP main effect
is ~0 (effect AT THE AVERAGE CITY, because the characteristics are
mean-centered), while run 02 gives -0.18 (a treated-weighted average).
These agree precisely because CP adopters are above average on the
characteristics with negative interactions -- a coherence check that
passes, not a discrepancy.

5. SUPPORT AND SAMPLE (tab_treatment_counts.csv)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
183 cities; 68 ever CP, 70 ever LEZ, 28 ever both; 281 city-years with both
policies active. Enough overlap to identify the interaction, but note that
the synergy estimate leans on only 28 cities.

6. BOTTOM LINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Internally the pipeline is consistent and robust: parametrizations agree,
  learners agree, folds agree, mediators behave as theory predicts, and the
  heterogeneity run coheres with the main run.
- Causally, the placebo test shows selection into treatment on high
  emission levels. Taken at face value the point estimates OVERSTATE the
  LEZ effect (its positive sign is likely pure selection) and UNDERSTATE
  the CP reduction. The report should present the main table together with
  the placebo table and interpret the estimates as biased bounds, not as
  clean causal effects. See discussion.txt for the full limitations
  discussion.
